## The Legal Landscape: Navigating Florida Regulations

Here is the thing: running a successful arbitrage business requires absolute legal compliance. A solid **sublease agreement** is mandatory. You cannot sneak behind a landlord's back; you must operate as a legitimate business.

Florida state law generally prevents local municipalities from outright banning short-term rentals, which is a massive win for investors. However, local cities *can* heavily regulate them.

*   **Licensing is Mandatory:** The state of Florida requires a vacation rental license through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR).
*   **Tax Collection:** You must collect and remit both the state sales tax (6%) and local county bed taxes (which vary, often around 5-6%). While platforms like Airbnb usually collect these automatically, you must ensure your business is properly registered.
*   **HOA Restrictions:** This is the biggest hurdle. If you are leasing an apartment in a complex with a Homeowners Association, you must read the bylaws carefully. Many HOAs mandate minimum lease terms of 6 or 12 months, effectively banning standard Airbnb operations.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can landlords legally say no to rental arbitrage in Florida?
Yes. A landlord has complete control over their property. If their standard lease prohibits subletting, you cannot run an arbitrage business there. You must negotiate a specific addendum or a commercial lease that explicitly grants you permission to operate a short-term rental.

### Do I need an LLC to do arbitrage in Florida?
It is highly recommended. Operating your arbitrage business under a Florida LLC protects your personal assets and makes you appear much more credible when pitching your business model to large apartment complexes or property management firms.

### How much money do I need to start?
To be safe, you should have between $8,000 and $12,000 in capital for your first unit in Florida.
